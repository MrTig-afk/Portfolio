## Problem

Monthly bank exports are the only honest record of where money goes, but every budgeting app wants your bank login or your raw statements on its servers.

## What I built

A self-hosted personal finance tracker that turns monthly CommBank and Westpac CSV exports into an AI-categorised spending dashboard, on desktop and as an installable phone PWA. Privacy is a property of the architecture, not a promise.

## Architecture

One FastAPI backend owns all data and logic; the desktop dashboard and the phone PWA are stateless views of it, served privately over Tailscale. A mandatory sanitiser runs before any network call and fails closed: if a row cannot be confidently cleaned, it is dropped rather than sent. The only payload that leaves is an anonymous description and amount, and every outbound payload is written to a local audit log.

## Key decisions

- **Idempotent pipeline.** File and transaction fingerprinting make re-runs a no-op: no duplicate rows and no wasted categorisation calls.
- **Internal transfer netting.** Money moved between your own accounts is matched as opposite-sign pairs and excluded from totals and from the categorisation payload.
- **Content-based bank detection.** Files are matched to a bank by their contents, not their upload slot; unknown formats are rejected with a clear message.
- **Always-on supervisor.** A small Windows service probes both servers every 15 seconds, relaunches whichever is down, and snapshots the database weekly.

## Results

Seven shipped versions, two banks parsed, and 100% local data. The demo on this site is the same frontend fed entirely by synthetic data.
